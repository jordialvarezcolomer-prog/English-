# Production plan — "Bench dips vs bar dips" (UK English pilot, Alex vs Daniel)

**Source:** McKenzie A, Crowley-McHattan Z, Meir R, Whitting J, Volschenk W. *Bench, Bar, and Ring Dips: Do Kinematics and Muscle Activity Differ?* IJERPH 2022;19(20):13211, doi:10.3390/ijerph192013211. Checked facts: 13 experienced male lifters; mean peak triceps activation 0.83 ± 0.34 mV (bench), 1.04 ± 0.27 (bars), 1.05 ± 0.40 (rings), bench significantly lower; peak shoulder extension bench 88.1°, bar 78.2°, rings 61.7°; chest/back/biceps differ between bar and ring (direction not verified → narration only says "the difference shows up"). Not measured: growth. Part-2 hook: Crowley-McHattan et al., IJERPH 2022;19(21):14390 (fatigue).

**A. Central idea.** Same-looking exercise, very different triceps demand: bars > bench (~20 % more peak activation), but it is one session of EMG, not growth.
**B. Main curiosity.** "Which dip hits your triceps harder — and by how much?" Answer at 0:16–0:23; the honest limit ("activation ≠ growth") is the late twist.
**C. Hooks.** (1) Chosen: split screen Alex on bench vs Daniel on bars, two empty HP bars, line "Bench dips or bar dips? One of them hits your triceps a lot harder." (2) cold open on the number 0.83 vs 1.04. (3) "Your bench dips are wasting time" (rejected: overclaims).
