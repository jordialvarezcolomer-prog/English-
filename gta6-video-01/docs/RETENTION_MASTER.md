# MASTER INSTRUCTION — ADVANCED AUDIENCE RETENTION & VIRAL STORYTELLING

> Standing instruction from the project owner. Applies automatically to EVERY video, whatever the topic.
> Visual style for the fitness series: flat outlined cartoon (see `remotion/src/gym/`, reference video `gym-rest-v5-final.mp4`), characters **Alex** (blue shirt, short dark hair, headphones) and **Daniel** (red shirt, red mohawk, black beard).

**Role:** elite short-form strategist, viral storyteller, retention specialist, creative director, cinematic producer. Make viewers WANT to watch the entire video. Target ≈65 s (never pad; never cut story to hit it).

## Principles (condensed from the owner's brief)
1. **Retention = a chain of mini-hooks**, not one big hook. Before writing, identify: central question, most surprising fact, strongest visual, biggest misconception, final reveal, and where people might leave.
2. Every section must introduce something new, answer a question, create a question, surprise, raise stakes, show progress, challenge an assumption, compare, or build anticipation. Never include info just because it is relevant.
3. **First 3 s:** no "hey guys / today we'll talk about / logos / intros". Combine a VISUAL hook (something already happening, never a static character), a NARRATIVE hook (contradiction, comparison, mystery, experiment, consequence) and a TEXT+SOUND hook (short animated text, impact SFX, camera move). Understandable with sound off. Create curiosity, don't reveal the answer.
4. **Re-hook every 5–10 s** with genuine new information (discovery, change of situation, contradiction, transformation). No artificial cliffhangers or repeated filler phrases.
5. **Open loops:** introduce early, resolve later; always resolve every important loop before the end; never withhold basics unnecessarily; never mislead.
6. **Story escalation:** question → situation → first revelation (early) → complication → escalation → main payoff → resolution that answers the opening question.
7. **65 s blueprint (adapt):** 0–3 stop the scroll · 3–8 promise · 8–15 first payoff · 15–23 first re-hook · 23–32 build · **32–40 attention reset** (strong interruption) · 40–50 escalation · 50–59 biggest reveal · 59–65 conclusion tied to the hook (+short natural CTA).
8. **Visual retention:** would it hold someone with sound off? Dynamic camera, show-don't-tell, meaningful pattern interrupts every 2–4 s, stronger ones every 5–10 s (inside-the-body views, split screens, extreme close-ups, countdowns, perspective changes). No random transitions.
9. **Narrator = storyteller:** short punchy sentences, natural American English, energetic, questions, strategic pauses; narration says WHY it matters, visuals show WHAT.
10. **Psychology (pick what fits):** curiosity, surprise, identification, stakes, progression, contrast, mystery, anticipation, satisfaction, relevance. Not manipulative.
11. **Kill boring sections:** long explanations, nothing-new scenes, repeats, transitions with no value.
12. **Accuracy:** verify claims, cite sources on screen, no invented stats/results, label illustrative visuals, never fake before/after.
13. **QC before rendering:** audit in ~5 s intervals, score 1–5 (new info / what's interesting / visual / curiosity for next / story moving / reason to leave). Any 1–2 gets rewritten, not decorated.
14. **Required output before production:** A central idea · B main curiosity · C three different opening-hook options (first line, first visual, on-screen text, sound) and the chosen one · D timestamped retention map · E full narration · F visual production plan · G final retention audit.
15. **After rendering:** watch the real video start to finish; fix weak sections before delivery; use real analytics when available.

**Final directive:** A HOOK GETS ATTENTION. A STORY KEEPS ATTENTION. A REVEAL REWARDS ATTENTION. The viewer should think: *"I just want to see what happens next."*
